About the app

Two taps from a photo to the shelf it's sitting on.

NearBuyGoods is an API-first storefront for what is actually on the shelves near you. One JSON service powers the iOS and Android apps — so the same search, prices and reservations are on every phone.

Cutting-edge where it counts

Zero-dependency Node service, 33 documented REST endpoints, JWT auth, rate limits, geohash indexing, Web Push and a Paystack checkout flow.

A real market to play in

Every install ships with a fictional Lagos market — 8 stores, 28 products, 74 live offers, deals, reviews and a reservation queue — so you can try the whole loop without setting anything up.

  • Guest mode for browsing — no account needed to search and compare.
  • Works offline — cached catalogue reads and a saved watchlist.
  • Built for 44 px thumbs: bottom tab bar, centre camera button, bottom sheets.

How it works

Four steps from “I want this” to “it's behind the counter”

01

Capture the item

Photo, barcode, voice, link or plain text. The recognizer scores every signal against the catalogue and returns ranked candidates with confidence.

02

Match the signals

Barcode hits are decisive; name and brand tokens are weighted; colours count more when they're rare in the catalogue. You can see exactly what matched.

03

Compare nearby stores

Offers inside your radius, sorted by price or distance, with stock, condition, hours, pickup options and an eight-week price history.

04

Reserve & get alerts

Hold the item with a pickup code, then let the watchlist do the work: price-drop and back-in-stock push notifications land even with the app closed.

Still curious?

Everything below is verifiable in the repo — the API docs, the OpenAPI spec, the demo dataset, the Android guide and the deploy notes are all public.

Browse the API docs See the source Get the app
A storefront for what's already on the shelves around you. You upload or describe an item; the service identifies it, finds nearby stores holding it, compares their prices and stock, and lets you reserve it for pickup. The same REST API powers the iOS and Android apps.
The client extracts dominant colours and decodes barcodes when present; those signals go to POST /api/v1/recognize, which scores them against the catalogue. A barcode hit is decisive (0.95), name tokens weigh most, and colour matches count more when the colour is rare in the catalogue. You get ranked candidates with a confidence score and the evidence list (matched_on) so you can see why something surfaced.
Not to look around — guest mode covers search, discovery, product pages and price history. An account (email + password, or the OTP flow) unlocks saving, collections, reservations, alerts and receipts. In demo mode you can sign in instantly as shopper, store owner or admin.
An iPhone or an Android phone, once the app lands — it's in final review for the App Store and Google Play. The apps wrap the same interface with native camera, GPS, file-picker and download bridges, and talk to the same API.
Every install ships with a clearly labelled, fictional Lagos market: 8 stores, 28 products, 74 offers, deals, reviews and reservations. It's a working playground for the whole loop. Point the app at your own store data (or your own deployment) and the same flows apply to real listings.
Shoppers can search, compare, save and reserve on the free tier. Premium (₦1,500/month) adds unlimited searches, no ads, priority alerts and price-drop predictions; the Family plan (₦2,500/month) covers up to six people. Stores pay ₦15,000 or ₦35,000 a month depending on listing limits and analytics depth.
Register as a store owner, add your store's address, hours and pickup options, then load inventory with prices and stock. The dashboard tracks views, matches, clicks and reservations, and any price or stock change automatically notifies shoppers who are watching that item.
Your coordinates are used to compute distances inside your chosen radius — that's it. You can skip GPS entirely and type a location or drop a pin by hand, and the profile has one-tap GDPR export and account deletion. Passwords are hashed with scrypt, sessions are HMAC-signed JWTs, and every write is validated server-side.
